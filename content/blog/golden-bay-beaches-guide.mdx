Golden Bay’s coastline is one of the most diverse in Aotearoa New Zealand. From calm family-friendly bays to wild windswept stretches, every beach has its own character. Whether you’re here for swimming, walking, snorkelling, or simply soaking in the views, you’ll find a spot that feels like it was made just for you.

Think of this guide as a journey around the Bay: starting at the Abel Tasman National Park end, winding your way along the central bays, and finishing at the remote wild beaches to the west and south.

### A friendly note from the locals

Golden Bay’s beaches are home to a rich variety of wildlife — from nesting seabirds to little blue penguins and curious seals. Please help us protect them by keeping a respectful distance, avoiding nesting areas, and taking all rubbish with you.

## Wainui Beach

Just after you pass the Wainui sign on Abel Tasman Drive, you’ll find this wide sandy beach right next to the road. It’s one of the most beautiful and accessible spots in Golden Bay, and a local favourite for swimming and relaxing.

**How to get there:** Park on the side of the road just after the Wainui sign, then follow the short but relatively steep path down to the beach.

**Facilities:** No public toilets at this beach — nearest ones are at Wainui Bay car park a short drive further along.

**Tip:** Best enjoyed around mid to high tide for snorkelling, when the water is clear and full of marine life.

## Wainui Bay / Taupō Point

At the far western end of Wainui Bay, you’ll find the gateway to the Taupō Point walk. The track takes you along the beach with sweeping views, then over farmland and rocky sections to a headland that was once a Māori pā site.

**How to get there:** Turn off Abel Tasman Drive onto McShane Road and follow it to the end for the Wainui Bay car park (toilets and DOC information board). Cross the tidal flats at low tide, or take the bush track at high tide.

**Dog policy:** Dogs are not permitted on the Taupō Point track or within the Abel Tasman National Park boundary.

**Tip:** Wear sturdy footwear — parts of the track are rocky. Time your walk for low tide for the easiest access.

## Totaranui

Remote and breathtaking, Totaranui is a long, sweeping beach with famously golden sand. It’s popular with campers and trampers heading into the Abel Tasman, but never feels crowded.

**Facilities:** Public toilets, water, and picnic areas at the Totaranui DOC Campground.

**Dog policy:** Dogs are not permitted in Totaranui or within Abel Tasman National Park.

## Tata Beach

Arguably the most popular beach in Golden Bay, Tata is loved for its long stretch of golden sand and calm waters. Families, swimmers, and kayakers all flock here on sunny days.

**Facilities:** Public toilets near the main beach access by the boat ramp.

**Dog policy:** From 20 December to 20 January, dogs are allowed on the beach between 8:30 am – 10:00 am and 5:00 pm – sunset as a controlled exercise area. At other times of the year, standard dog control rules apply — keep dogs on a lead and under control.

**Tip:** Hire a kayak and explore the coastline from the water.

## Ligar Bay

Just around the corner from Tata, Ligar Bay has especially warm shallows, making it perfect for a relaxed swim. It’s a quieter option with a lot of charm.

**Facilities:** Public toilets near the centre of the bay.

**Dog policy:** Year-round controlled exercise area — dogs are welcome when under control.

**Tip:** Locals often hang swings in the trees on the eastern side — kids love them.

## Pohara Beach

The social heart of Golden Bay’s beach scene, Pohara stretches for kilometres and is perfect for long walks, beach cricket, or simply sitting and watching the tide roll in.

**Facilities:** Public toilets beside the campground playground.

**Dog policy:** From November to February (inclusive), dogs must be on leash between the eastern camp boundary and Selwyn Street Reserve, and from Selwyn Street to the western end of the golf course. The rest of the year, the beach is a controlled exercise area.

## Patons Rock Beach

A little gem offering calm shallows, safe swimming, and a sense of seclusion — a favourite with families who want a quieter alternative.

**Facilities:** Public toilets at the beach access car park.

**Dog policy:** Year-round controlled exercise area.

## Parapara Beach

Known for its wide beach and relaxed atmosphere, Parapara has a real locals-only feel.

**Facilities:** Public toilets beside the playground and picnic area at the end of Bishop Road.

**Dog policy:** Controlled exercise area year-round. Dogs are prohibited at all times within the Parapara Inlet and Milnthorpe Quay Reserve.

## Milnthorpe Beach

Near Collingwood, Milnthorpe is shaped by the tides, revealing endless sandflats and small pools — wonderful for birdwatching, walking, and exploring with children.

**Dog policy:** Dogs are prohibited at all times within the Milnthorpe Quay Reserve and adjacent inlet area.

## Collingwood Beaches

The beaches around Collingwood are wide, quiet, and perfect for an evening stroll, with peaceful views across the bay towards Farewell Spit.

**Facilities:** Public toilets at 9 Tasman Street and at the Collingwood Campground.

**Dog policy:** Controlled exercise area year-round; dogs are prohibited in the Collingwood Inlet and along the Aorere River mouth.

## Pakawau Beach

Pakawau has that classic New Zealand holiday vibe, with a friendly campground and a long beach perfect for walks and shell collecting.

**Facilities:** Public toilets at the Pakawau Beach Campground.

## Farewell Spit

Stretching 34 km into the sea, Farewell Spit (Onetahua) is one of Golden Bay’s most extraordinary natural landmarks — a protected wildlife sanctuary and vital habitat for migratory birds.

**How to get there:** Drive north from Collingwood through Puponga to the Farewell Spit car park. Guided eco-tours can take you further along the spit.

**Dog policy:** Dogs are prohibited on Farewell Spit and within the nature reserve.

## Wharariki Beach

No trip to Golden Bay is complete without Wharariki Beach — rolling dunes, dramatic sea arches, and often a colony of playful seal pups.

**Facilities:** Public toilets at the Wharariki Beach DOC car park.

**Dog policy:** Dogs are prohibited at all times at Wharariki Beach and within the Wharariki Recreation Reserve.

**Tip:** Go at low tide for the best access to the rock pools and sea arches.

## Anatori

Tucked away on the remote West Coast of Golden Bay, Anatori is less about swimming and more about the journey itself. The drive winds along the wild coastline before ending at the Anatori River and a rugged, untouched beach.

**Tip:** Plan this as a full-day adventure. A 4WD is recommended if you plan to cross the river or explore further south.
